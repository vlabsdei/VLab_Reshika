Phase 2

Mathematical Verification & Experimental Conclusions

1. Kinetic Energy (KE) Calculation

Kinetic energy represents the total energy possessed by the vehicle due to its motion. This is the maximum theoretical energy available for recovery during deceleration.

KE = 12 m v2

Inferred Values:

  • Vehicle Mass (m): 1500 kg (Constant)
  • Current Speed (v): 19.44 m/s (70 km/h)
Substitution: 0.5 × 1500 × (19.44)2
Result (Initial KE): 283,457 J

2. Regenerative Energy Recovery

The recovered energy depends on the initial kinetic energy and the final efficiency (η), which shifts dynamically based on slope, grip, and traffic flow.

Erecovered = KE × η

Inferred Values:

  • Initial Kinetic Energy (KE): 283,457 J
  • Regenerative Efficiency (η): 82.0%
Substitution: 283,457 J × 0.820
Result (Recovered Energy): 232,435 J

3. Battery Charge Gain (ΔSOC)

Calculates the percentage increase in the battery's State of Charge (SOC) by comparing recovered energy to the total battery capacity.

ΔSOC = ErecoveredEbattery × 100

Inferred Values:

  • Recovered Energy (Erecovered): 232,435 J
  • Battery Capacity (Ebattery): 216,000,000 J (60 kWh)
Substitution: 232,435 / 216,000,000 × 100
Result (SOC Gain): 0.1076%
STABLE

Experimental Conclusion

The vehicle was decelerated in a stable manner. Due to high surface grip, the braking force did not exceed the road traction limit, permitting full recovery efficiency.

Key Engineering Insights

  • Safety Margins: Maintaining braking force below road grip is critical to prevent wheel slip.
  • Environmental Influence: Road grade (slope) changes the normal load and gravity assistance, shifting the regen duration.
  • System Efficiency: Traffic density determines the level of continuous vs. transitionary brake blending.
